Plan · light tier · yacana-fidelity

Bring the three pages to the design binder's frames

The miner, the stats and the landing shipped with the binder's colours and components but not its page layouts. This plan makes the binder's frames the spec, rebuilds the stats charts on Observable Plot with hover tooltips and animated updates, and adds a screenshot test so the pages cannot drift again.

What went wrong, in numbers

The binder draws every page on an 1120-pixel canvas. The live pages were built narrower and stacked instead of gridded, which is why everything reads as cramped. The measurements below are copied from the binder's own stylesheet and are the plan's spec.

Why the light tier

Six risk dimensions, one high: external coupling (a new chart library). Novelty, blast radius, irreversibility, migration cost and security sensitivity are all low: the pages hold no keys during these layouts, nothing touches the chain or the reader, and every change is a revert away. A bounded, well-specified UI change is what the light tier is for.

The phases

Arc 1 · miner · one PR

P1.1 Shell width, tiles and tokens

Widen all three pages to the binder's 1120 pixels, give tiles the binder's padding, and correct two small token drifts (the eyebrow's size, the tile labels' tone). Every page inherits this.

proves itself by: lint · typecheck · every package's component tests green

P1.2 The cockpit grid

Lay the miner out exactly as the M1 frame: the loop across three columns with the epoch and power rail beside it, three KPI tiles under the loop, the proof ledger and the key tile in the last row. Between 900 and 1280 pixels the rail drops under the numbers, as the binder's own note says.

proves itself by: lint · typecheck · component tests · the 11 miner end-to-end specs on the isolated network

Arc 2 · landing · one PR

P2.1 The seven frames

Each section becomes its frame: the hero with the purple primary button and the wider headline column, the money rules beside the comparison table, the chain section with its three chips, the "how" cards, the live numbers in one row with the sparkline, the verify chips, the centred close.

proves itself by: lint · typecheck · component tests · the 3 landing end-to-end specs (including "nothing heavy loads before the click")

Arc 3 · stats · one PR

P3.1 The observatory grid

Six KPI tiles in one row, the epoch strip beside the epoch detail, four charts in a two-by-two block, the table, and the closing pair ("what is not here" beside a compact verify tile).

proves itself by: lint · typecheck · component tests

P3.2 Charts on Observable Plot

The four charts are rebuilt on Plot: hover any epoch for its numbers, a crosshair on the difficulty line, labelled lines for the expected duration and the escape hatch, the selected epoch highlighted, and a short animation when a new epoch arrives. Reduced-motion settings are respected.

proves itself by: lint · typecheck · rewritten component tests · the stats end-to-end specs · a production build showing Plot only under /stats/

P3.3 The screenshot gate

A test renders the stats page at 1280 and 1440 pixels against a frozen clock and compares it with committed reference images. A layout regression fails the test; an intended change updates the images in the same PR, reviewed like code.

proves itself by: the stats end-to-end run passing twice on the committed images · lint

How it ships

Three stacked PRs, one per page, opened only after each one's reviewer loop converged: Codex reviews every arc against the frame with the live and binder screenshots side by side, the fixes go back for re-review until a round finds nothing material, and a final fresh review looks across all three for seams. Each PR body carries the page at 1280 and 1440 beside the binder frame. You merge and deploy (bun run site:deploy); the agent never does.

What the reviewer found

Codex audited the plan twice. Round one rejected it (eight findings, all adopted); round two conditionally approved it (seven conditions, all adopted). The two that matter to you:

  • The stats page shares the miner's origin. A chart library is not "just a chart": a compromised release of Observable Plot would run with the same access as the miner, including the vault where a twelve-words key is sealed. The plan now says this plainly, keeps every supply-chain guard (seven-day minimum age, frozen lockfile, audit in CI, the lock diff reviewed), and puts "move stats and landing to their own host before mainnet" on the roadmap. You are asked to accept that risk knowingly, or to drop Plot and upgrade the hand-rolled charts instead.
  • The screenshot gate is now real. It runs on every stats PR without a node, inside the same pinned browser image locally and in CI, with zero tolerance, and the plan proves it bites by making three deliberate regressions fail before the first reference images are recorded.

Decisions you are asked to make

  • 1 · The wallet-origin risk. Accept Observable Plot on the stats page with the guards above, or choose the no-dependency route.
  • 2 · What "dynamic" means. Tooltips, a crosshair, labelled reference lines, the selected epoch highlighted, and a short cross-fade when a new epoch arrives. No continuous motion. Confirm this reading.
  • 3 · The in-between and phone layouts. The binder draws only the 1120-pixel frames. Below that the pages use the agent's judgment (the miner's rail drops under the numbers; the stats KPIs go two-by-three, then one column on phones; the landing frames stack), reviewed by Codex on screenshots. You see them in every PR at 1280, 1440, 1024 and 390 pixels.
  • 4 · Who owns the reference images. Whoever changes the page updates them in the same PR; the first set is judged against the binder in the stats review.
