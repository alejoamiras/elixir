The node, the waits, the strip, the pop-out

You picked the screens on the design canvas. This plan turns those picks into three pull requests: a node you can change from the miner's settings with a banner when it throttles; a stats page that shows its shape at once and fills as the numbers land; a miner that shows the chain before you sign in and opens your account behind a small window with a real progress bar; an epochs strip that stays readable at a thousand epochs; and the four small things (the senders card out, the pop-out redrawn, the money table's last line, the label that collided). Two independent reviewers audit it, then a fresh third pass; every finding is folded in before you see this.

Why this tier

Rubric: novelty low (every piece extends a surface that exists), blast radius high (the node setting and its security policy touch all three pages and the wallet), irreversibility low (pages, no chain, no data), migration low (one new browser cache, dropped whole if it is ever wrong), external coupling medium (public nodes and their rate limits), security sensitivity high in one line (the page may now talk to any https node). Two highs would say deep; both come from the one decision you already took (any https node), argued in the plan's security section and attacked by both audits, so mid with the full dual audit is the honest fit. Budget: one recon agent, code-review off, codex loops per arc plus a final cross-arc pass.

The picks, as the plan reads them

Node
Any https node, typed into a field; checked against this deployment before it is used; "Use this node" applies at once: mining pauses for a moment, the page's handle on the node moves, your account's view of the chain is rebuilt from the new node (about a minute, shown in the small window), and mining carries on (your decision A4; the reviewers leaned to a reload, the reasons are in the plan). The Aztec Labs node stays the default. The miner and token addresses stop being settings. If a saved node is dead, every page offers "Use the default node".
Throttled
A banner under the header on all three pages: what is wrong, how old the numbers are, what still works, the retry countdown, and "Use another node" one click away. It goes when the node answers again.
Stats
The page's structure from the first frame; grey shimmer where numbers and plots go (no shimmer if you asked your system for less motion); nothing shown under 300 ms; the first numbers in about a second, the history a few seconds later.
Mine
Signed out, the cockpit shows everything the chain gives, dimmed, with "Sign in to mine" where Start was; the sign-in is a small window over the blurred page, with "Not now — just watch" and the same links today's screens have (see A5). Opening an account keeps the window up with a bar and named steps (option A); Cancel really cancels.
Strip
Stays 48 epochs wide. Under it a map of every epoch ever; the window is drawn on the map; drag it or page with ‹ ›, and the table, the charts and the link follow (option A). The map fills in the background a page at a time and is remembered in the browser; if the node throttles, the filling stops for that visit and the unread part shows as a hairline rather than raising the banner itself.
Senders
Gone, card and capability (your decision A1). The browser test then showed it had cost nothing: a private transfer between two Yacana accounts arrives with no sender registered (Aztec's first-contact handshake does that), so no warning was needed either.
Small fixes
The pop-out draws proofs as ticks like the cockpit and never cuts the bar's number; the money table's last row loses its line; the "0" you saw is most likely the pop-out clipping a two-digit bar into "0.0", or the bar's label sitting on the baseline's label when the bar is at the floor — the plan reproduces it before calling it fixed, and both surfaces stop drawing that.

Human context

  • Why the policy line changes. Today the page may only talk to the one node baked into the build; that is what "any node" costs. The page still runs no script but its own, and the wallet's real threat (a script already on the page) was never held back by that line. Both audits were asked to attack this.
  • Why the live switch rebuilds the view. Both reviewers read the wallet library: it caches what it learned from the first node, and its view of the chain is kept per network, not per node, so a node that is behind, or lying, can wipe or poison that view. The switch therefore pauses mining, waits for anything in flight to finish, moves the handle, rebuilds the view from the new node (the same routine the miner already uses after a lost race), and resumes. The reviewers preferred a reload; you chose the switch in place, and the plan proves it between two real endpoints in the browser suite.
  • Why the page guards its own requests. With any https node allowed, the browser policy no longer blocks stray requests, and one safety net relied on that (the proving keys must never come from the network unverified). The page now refuses in code every request that is not to itself or to the node you chose, and the same guard answers "slow down" locally while a node is rate-limiting, so nothing keeps hammering it.
  • Why the map needs a memory. Reading one epoch is three requests; a thousand epochs is three thousand. The page reads them once, in the background, and keeps closed epochs in the browser (they never change), so the map is whole from the second visit on.
  • Why the keys download moves. The 20 MB used to sit in front of the sign-in screen. Starting it at page load lets it run while you read the window; if it is done by the time you sign in, the step shows a tick.

What the reviewers said

Two reviewers read the first draft against the code and the wallet library, then a third read the finished plan fresh with the whole argument trail.

  • Codex, first read: reject. The security argument for "any https node" was wrong in one place and too kind in another; the proving-key safety net leaned on the old policy; a live node switch under the wallet was unproven; "health" measured the connection, not the numbers; Cancel had no owner; the browser tests would have been blocked by the new policy. Every point taken; the live switch became save-and-reload.
  • Fable, first read: approve with nine conditions. Went further on the wallet: a node switch, even by reload, can leave the account's view of the chain pruned or poisoned unless it is rebuilt from the new node; the throttle guard should also answer the wallet's own requests; rate limits mostly arrive as opaque network errors; keep the sign-in screens' links; a few facts and sizes. Every condition taken.
  • Codex, rounds two to four on the revision: caught what my edits had left half-done (the same node on two paths, the migration of your registered senders across a rebuild, one caller at a time on recovery, the map's last cell, the exact list of sign-in links) and ended at "approve with two conditions", both taken.
  • Codex, fresh read of the finished plan: reject on two facts about the wallet library (the request guard would have blocked the prover's own bundled code; the sender migration read the wrong list) — both verified against the library and fixed — then approve on the follow-up, with one wording note, applied.
