tl;dr

We want people to optimize the prover. So let's pay them for it.

Everybody says "someone should make client-side proving faster". Nobody wakes up wanting to shave 8% off sumcheck. Bitcoin solved the equivalent problem for SHA256 by making the hash the thing that pays. Elixir does that for Barretenberg: the only way to earn ELX is to produce more accepting UltraHonk proofs per hour than the next person. Better algorithms, SIMD, a GPU prover, whatever. All of it is "mining".

It's a research PoC, Aztec-native, private by default, and the mechanism is the hard part. The UX is not. So this pitch is 90% mechanism.

the trap

Anything you can check natively, people will grind natively

We had a v0 from the RIF era. It hashed (seed, epoch, slot, nonce, miner) with Poseidon2, and a public AVM function re-hashed once and minted if the digest was under a target. Cute, but the work never touched the prover: the miner runs Poseidon2 natively a billion times and the chain checks one. That is a CUDA kernel contest. Ingonyama already ships Poseidon2 kernels. Nobody optimizes bb to win that.

The fix is a trick Aleo landed on years ago: make the ticket the hash of the proof itself. To know whether a nonce wins, you must finish a proof. Losers never touch the chain.

the loop

Mining, one nonce at a time

params = ElixirMiner.epoch_params(e)            // seed_e, target_e, claims_e  (public reads)
commit = Poseidon2(DOM_SECRET, secret)          // per-epoch random secret, never leaves the page
loop while claims_e < 24:
  nonce += 1
  witness = W(domain, seed_e, e, commit, nonce) // W = Poseidon2 chain, ~150k gates. Boring on purpose.
  proof   = UltraHonk.prove(W, witness)         // the work. bb.js WASM in the browser, native bb later
  digest  = Poseidon2(DOM_TICKET, proof[0..410])
  if low128(digest) < target_e: claim(e, nonce, out, secret, proof)   // rare

Nobody searches for anything inside W. Native evaluation of the chain takes microseconds; proving it takes seconds. Faster Poseidon2 buys you nothing. Faster proving buys you tickets. The nonce is absorbed first so wire polynomials cannot be reused across nonces by an honest prover.

difficulty, epochs, emission

It's Bitcoin, with claims instead of blocks

Bitcoin: hash below target, 2016-block window, retarget by actual/expected time clamped to ×4, fixed subsidy. Ours: ticket below a 128-bit target, an epoch is 24 accepted claims, retarget by actual/expected time clamped to ¼…4, fixed 4 ELX per claim. Counting claims instead of hours is what caps issuance: a 100× hashrate surge makes epochs short, never fat. Every closed epoch mints 96 ELX and only 24 transactions land, no matter how many GPUs show up.

privacy

Recipient-private, not "nobody can tell it's mining"

Private

  • Who mined. The commitment inside W is H(secret); the secret is random per epoch and dies with it.
  • Balances. mint_to_private on the aztec-standards token, v5.2.0.
  • The proof and the nonce. They never leave the miner; the claim proves knowledge of them.

Public

  • That a claim happened, its epoch, the winning digest (pseudorandom), and 4 ELX.
  • That the tx is a mining claim (function identity, public effects).
  • Timing. RPC-level linkage is the miner's own problem, as in any Aztec app.

the honest part

What we're explicitly accepting, and where I want your eyes

The guarantee is "one fresh accepting transcript per ticket", not "one honest proof per ticket". Non-ZK UltraHonk disables the first 4 sumcheck rows (σ = id there). A modified prover can re-prove the same witness with fresh junk in those 16 cells, update the wire commitments with ~16 scalar mults instead of size-n MSMs, and get a fresh ticket for roughly half to two-thirds of an honest prove. Still a full sumcheck and a KZG opening per ticket, still scales with circuit size, but it's a constant factor available to anyone who patches bb. Phase 1 builds the attack and measures it. bb people: if constraining those rows in the non-ZK flavour is a 20-line upstream change, tell me.

  • Escape-hatch seeds are predictable. When an epoch closes by the 4-hour timeout, every seed input is public in advance. Only reachable under collapse; simulated, accepted.
  • The deployer can pre-mine epoch 0. Addresses and constructor args are precomputable. Count-capping makes that worth 96 ELX. Disclosed.
  • Winning tickets near an epoch's end go stale. Roughly 8% at N=24 with 1–2 min of claim proving. Bitcoin has orphans; we have this.
  • No external audit, no mainnet in this plan. Testnet, embedded wallet, sponsored fees. The proof-system assumptions rest on measurements and on whoever here is willing to poke at them.
  • Deployment needed a trick. Aztec addresses commit to constructor args, so a miner that takes the token and a token that takes the miner is a fixed point no salt solves. The miner is deployed token-less and bound once by the deployer; the setup authority then expires.
plan

Five phases, one hard stop

  1. 0
    Scaffold.

    Bun workspace, Biome, CI gates, isolated local sandbox runs. Boring, one day.

  2. 1
    Spike, then stop.

    Work circuit at three sizes, proof-layout manifest, mutation tests through the recursive path, a real claim through an embedded wallet on a sandbox, ECCVM rows, in-browser time and memory, the disabled-row attack cost. Written GO / NO-GO. Nothing else starts until that's read.

  3. 2
    Contracts.

    Miner (claims, count-capped close, mulDiv retarget with range-checked division, escape hatch, one-shot token bind), aztec-standards token, Noir tests plus a retarget simulator with surges, collapse and a strategic withholder.

  4. 3
    Miner core.

    Platform-agnostic TypeScript: prove, hash, check, claim. Fixtures pin every hash to the Noir side. Real-proving integration on a sandbox, including a public-effects inspection that asserts exactly what leaks.

  5. 4·5
    Web miner, then a testnet soak.

    React + bb.js in a Worker, embedded wallet, sponsored fees. Then 24+ epochs on testnet with miners started and stopped so the target actually moves. Native mining via aztec-accelerator comes after, on the same core.

Fallbacks if phase 1 says no: an L1 portal that verifies the winning proof with a generated Solidity verifier and messages Aztec (keeps the lottery), or pay-per-proof with a reward that auto-adjusts (drops the lottery, every proof is a tx). I'd rather not.

What I want from you

  • Break the ticket. If you know a cheaper way to get a second accepting Honk transcript than the disabled-row trick, I want it now, not on testnet.
  • Opinions on W. A Poseidon2 chain measures the general prover path. Should the work look more like a real Aztec tx (Merkle paths, notes) so optimizations transfer 1:1?
  • ECCVM headroom and the Chonk tail: anything about stacking a recursive verification inside an app circuit that will bite us?
  • And obviously: if someone here ends up writing a faster prover to farm ELX, that is the project succeeding.
